Absolutely. For **quant trading**, the mathematical stack is much deeper than VC. A useful one-pager is essentially the map from **probability → statistics → stochastic processes → optimization → pricing → execution**. # Quant Trading Mathematics — One-Pager ## 1. Probability ### Conditional probability $$ P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)} $$ ### Bayes' theorem $$ P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)} $$ ### Expectation $$ E[X]=\sum_x xP(X=x) $$ or continuously: $$ E[X]=\int x f(x)\,dx $$ ### Variance $$ Var(X)=E[(X-E[X])^2] $$ $$ \sigma=\sqrt{Var(X)} $$ ### Covariance $$ Cov(X,Y)=E[(X-E[X])(Y-E[Y])] $$ ### Correlation $$ \rho_{XY}= \frac{Cov(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} $$ **Quant intuition:** correlation measures co-movement; covariance also depends on scale. --- # 2. Statistics ### Sample mean $$ \bar{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i $$ ### Standard error $$ SE(\bar{x})=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n} $$ ### Z-score $$ Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma} $$ ### Linear regression $$ Y=\alpha+\beta X+\epsilon $$ where $$ \beta=\frac{Cov(X,Y)}{Var(X)} $$ and $$ \alpha=E[Y]-\beta E[X] $$ ### Sharpe ratio $$ Sharpe=\frac{E[R-R_f]}{\sigma_R} $$ For daily returns annualized approximately: $$ Sharpe_{annual}\approx Sharpe_{daily}\sqrt{252} $$ --- # 3. Time Series For a price \(P_t\), log return: $$ r_t=\ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right) $$ ### Random walk $$ P_t=P_{t-1}+\epsilon_t $$ ### AR(1) $$ X_t=c+\phi X_{t-1}+\epsilon_t $$ Mean reversion generally requires: $$ |\phi|<1 $$ ### Autocorrelation $$ \rho_k=Corr(X_t,X_{t-k}) $$ This is fundamental for determining whether past observations contain predictive information. --- # 4. Brownian Motion A standard Brownian motion \(W_t\) satisfies: $$ W_{t+\Delta t}-W_t\sim N(0,\Delta t) $$ and $$ E[W_t]=0 $$ $$ Var(W_t)=t $$ A key scaling law: $$ \boxed{\text{volatility}\propto\sqrt{t}} $$ --- # 5. Stochastic Calculus ### Geometric Brownian Motion A classic model for asset prices: $$ dS_t=\mu S_tdt+\sigma S_tdW_t $$ where: * \(S_t\) = price * \(\mu\) = drift * \(\sigma\) = volatility * \(W_t\) = Brownian motion ### Itô's Lemma For $$ dS=\mu Sdt+\sigma SdW $$ and \(f(S,t)\): $$ df= \left( f_t+\mu Sf_S+\frac12\sigma^2S^2f_{SS} \right)dt +\sigma Sf_SdW $$ This is one of the mathematical foundations of quantitative finance. --- # 6. Options / Derivatives Black-Scholes: $$ C=SN(d_1)-Ke^{-rT}N(d_2) $$ where $$ d_1= \frac{\ln(S/K)+(r+\sigma^2/2)T} {\sigma\sqrt T} $$ $$ d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt T $$ The important Greeks: $$ \Delta=\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} $$ $$ \Gamma=\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial S^2} $$ $$ \Theta=\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} $$ $$ Vega=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\sigma} $$ $$ \rho=\frac{\partial V}{\partial r} $$ --- # 7. Linear Algebra Quant trading is heavily dependent on matrices. ### Portfolio return $$ R_p=w^TR $$ ### Portfolio variance $$ \sigma_p^2=w^T\Sigma w $$ where \(\Sigma\) is the covariance matrix. ### Eigenvalue decomposition $$ \Sigma=Q\Lambda Q^T $$ This connects directly to: * PCA * factor models * risk decomposition * dimensionality reduction * statistical arbitrage --- # 8. Optimization ### Mean-variance optimization $$ \max_w \left( w^T\mu-\frac{\lambda}{2}w^T\Sigma w \right) $$ subject to constraints such as: $$ \sum_iw_i=1 $$ and $$ w_i\geq0 $$ depending on the strategy. ### Sharpe maximization $$ \max_w \frac{w^T\mu}{\sqrt{w^T\Sigma w}} $$ --- # 9. Machine Learning / Statistical Learning Know: ### Bias-variance tradeoff $$ Expected\ Error = Bias^2+Variance+Noise $$ ### Loss functions MSE: $$ L=\frac1n\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2 $$ Log loss: $$ L=-\sum_i [y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)] $$ ### Regularization Ridge: $$ \min_\beta \left[ ||y-X\beta||^2+\lambda||\beta||^2 \right] $$ Lasso: $$ \min_\beta \left[ ||y-X\beta||^2+\lambda||\beta||_1 \right] $$ --- # 10. Information Theory Very useful for signal research. ### Entropy $$ H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x) $$ ### Mutual information $$ I(X;Y)= \sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)} $$ This can measure nonlinear dependence between potential signals and future returns. --- # 11. Hypothesis Testing Null hypothesis: $$ H_0:\theta=\theta_0 $$ Test statistic: $$ t= \frac{\hat\theta-\theta_0} {SE(\hat\theta)} $$ You need to understand: * p-values * confidence intervals * statistical power * Type I error * Type II error * multiple testing ### Multiple testing matters enormously If you test hundreds of strategies, some will appear profitable **by chance**. That is why quant researchers care about: $$ \boxed{\text{Out-of-sample performance}} $$ and $$ \boxed{\text{Multiple-hypothesis correction}} $$ --- # 12. Risk ### Value at Risk $$ VaR_\alpha $$ is the loss threshold exceeded with probability \(1-\alpha\) over a specified horizon. ### Expected Shortfall $$ ES_\alpha=E[L\mid L>VaR_\alpha] $$ Expected Shortfall asks: > **When things go beyond VaR, how bad are they on average?** --- # 13. Market Microstructure For actual trading, mathematics meets the order book. Know: $$ Spread=Ask-Bid $$ $$ Mid=\frac{Bid+Ask}{2} $$ ### Market impact A simplified model: $$ Impact\propto \sigma\sqrt{\frac{Q}{V}} $$ where: * \(Q\) = order size * \(V\) = trading volume * \(\sigma\) = volatility You also need: * limit vs market orders * bid/ask spread * liquidity * slippage * adverse selection * queue position * execution probability --- # The Quant Math Hierarchy ### Level 1 — Foundation **Calculus + Linear Algebra + Probability + Statistics** ↓ ### Level 2 — Quantitative Finance **Time Series + Optimization + Stochastic Processes** ↓ ### Level 3 — Trading Mathematics **Factor Models + Portfolio Theory + Derivatives + Risk + Microstructure** ↓ ### Level 4 — Quant Research **Statistical Learning + Bayesian Methods + Signal Processing + Information Theory** ↓ ### Level 5 — Advanced Quant **Stochastic Control + PDEs + Measure-Theoretic Probability + Numerical Methods + Optimal Execution** --- ## The core mental model A quant is ultimately trying to solve: $$ \boxed{ \text{Data} \rightarrow \text{Signal} \rightarrow \text{Expected Return} \rightarrow \text{Risk} \rightarrow \text{Position} \rightarrow \text{Execution} \rightarrow \text{P\&L} } $$ And the central mathematical question is: $$ \boxed{ E[R_{t+1}\mid X_t] \;>\; \text{Risk + Costs + Noise}? } $$ If you can move comfortably between **probability, statistics, linear algebra, optimization, stochastic processes, and market microstructure**, you have the mathematical foundation for serious quant research.
